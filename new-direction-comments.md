# new-direction.html — what fights the premium goal

Rysing sells 20–30k projects. The page has to carry that before anyone reads a
word. These are the things working against it, in order of damage.

---

## 1. Photography quality — replacements coming from the client

The lead panel of Selected Work is a blurry phone snapshot of a banner on
asphalt with a shoe in frame. Projects 05 and 06 are soft and out of focus. 02
and 04 are sharp studio work. A buyer registers this in half a second. No
typography survives it.

Decision: new photography is coming. Until it lands, keep the current files and
build the layout so the weakest images sit in the smallest slots and every image
is swappable without touching the design. See `ASSET-SPEC.md` for what to shoot.

## 2. Showreel content — PARKED, keep as is

The reel opens on brush-script and cuts between neon yellow, cobalt and orange
cards. It has no relationship to the restrained black-and-didone page around it.
Framing it contained the damage.

Decision: keep the current reel and the aperture. Not an action item.

## 3. Every section is the same volume

Fourteen sections, each one a large headline, a hairline, and a device. Nothing
is quiet, so nothing lands. The agencies this direction borrows from spend most
of the page near-silent so two or three moments can detonate. We inverted that
ratio.

## 4. Devices used without reason

The Kinfolk/Aileron alternation runs through every headline, so it has stopped
being a signal and become texture. The gallery offsets are arbitrary — panel
size does not track project importance. The service numerals are decoration.
Borrowed form without the reason behind it reads as imitation.

## 5. Proof is placeholder-grade

Six testimonials from "Marcus H." and "Elena V." with no faces, no company
marks, no figures. For a 30k decision this is the highest-leverage section on
the page and it currently reads as filler.

## 6. Work shows names, not outcomes

"Speaker and Investor Persona Brand — Gerd Bommer" says nothing about what
changed. Six uneven projects dilute each other. Three strong ones with real
results would do more.

## 7. The newsletter section undercuts selectivity

A rotated white card reading "Fudge for founders" is a creator-brand move. A
newsletter capture competing with the main CTA signals volume, not selection.

## 8. The CTA is repeated, not qualified

"Apply to work with us" appears five times in the same pill. Repetition reads as
chasing. Selective studios ask once and set a bar.

## 9. Red and blue appear as ambient haze

Both brand colours are used almost entirely as large blurred glows — hero,
stats, footer. That is a generic gradient treatment. Used rarely and hard, as
ink or a flooded surface, they would carry far more weight.

## 10. Four equal stats

35+ / 350+ / 3000+ / 50k+ sit in a neutral row at equal size. Nothing is the
headline figure.

## 11. No craft layer

Standard pills, standard hairlines, one uniform 0.45s ease on everything, no
considered cursor or hover states, no visible grid system, no editorial captions
or indexing. The close-range detail that signals obsession is missing.

## 12. The page is a brochure, not an argument

Order is: reel, numbers, services, work, founder, team, logos, reviews,
spotlight, newsletter, CTA. That is a list of contents. It does not build a case
for why this studio operates at the buyer's level.
